> **Research conditions (read first).** The session's network blocked direct page fetches from animalflow.com, gmb.io, humankinetics.com, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and mikereinold.com. All cited findings below come from search-result abstracts and snippets, not full texts. Treat exact numbers as "per abstract". **The per-movement doses, progressions and pass criteria in the catalogue (Q1 Inferences) are a coaching-informed synthesis. No source I could reach gave them, so they need coach or physio review before shipping.** Each item is labelled **[EVIDENCE]** (peer-reviewed data), **[GUIDELINE]** (clinical or professional body), **[COACHING]** (practitioner or commercial opinion) or **[SYNTHESIS]** (my inference).

## 1. Per-movement purpose, best slot, dose and progression

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence on these movements is thin. One RCT of a commercial quadrupedal movement programme (Animal Flow-style) showed better FMS scores and joint ROM. EMG studies show crawling and bear variants activate the core as much as or more than planks and push-ups, and that travelling or limb-lift bear variants beat a static bear hold. Everything about slotting, dose and level criteria is coaching convention. The Animal Flow curriculum's own order (wrist prep → static Beast/Crab "activations" → travelling forms → switches/transitions → flows) maps cleanly onto Raise/Move/Prime, rest-day flows and snacks.

### Cited Findings
- **[EVIDENCE]** An RCT of a commercial quadrupedal movement training (QMT) programme measured functional movement, dynamic balance, ROM and upper-body strength/endurance. The authors report that QMT can improve FMS scores and several active joint ranges of motion (Buxton et al.) — [PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/); [Animal Flow-hosted PDF](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/The_Effects_of_a_Novel_Quadrupedal_Movement.94204.pdf)
- **[EVIDENCE / industry-linked]** Animal Flow describes a 4-week study of 42 recreationally active young adults new to QMT, randomised into two groups. The authors said the results should be extended to other populations such as older adults and athletes — [search summary of animalflow.com "science behind Animal Flow"](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/science-behind-animal-flow/). Industry-funded, and it covers young healthy adults only.
- **[EVIDENCE]** Buxton et al. 2024 (*J Bodywork & Movement Therapies*, doi 10.1016/j.jbmt.2024.11.002) found muscle activation during QMT exercises (bear plank, bear crawl and others) comparable to or higher than push-ups, squats and forearm planks. The authors suggest QMT can train several muscle groups at once — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40NvVvP9); secondary summary at [Peloton](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/bear-plank). Peloton also says the bear crawl beat dead bugs and toe taps for transverse abdominis. That claim is secondary and unverified.
- **[EVIDENCE]** In a surface-EMG study of static, stationary and travelling trunk exercises (rectus abdominis, external oblique, erector spinae, rectus femoris), the bear limb lift and bear crawl activated every muscle tested more than the static bear hold — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318471304_Effects_of_Static_Stationary_and_Traveling_Trunk_Exercises_on_Muscle_Activation)
- **[EVIDENCE]** A study of 21 men compared serratus anterior and trapezius activity in push-up plus and the dynamic bear crawl. Only the title and abstract were visible, so the results could not be confirmed — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358692914_Activity_of_Different_Shoulder_Muscles_during_Dynamic_Bear_Crawl_Exercise)
- **[EVIDENCE]** A 2024 study of 44 young adults found bird dog and side plank done with abdominal hollowing gave the largest gains in muscle thickness. It also tested a beast crawl — [JFMK via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/8864aa6a1965413fae20e2d7380b474b)
- **[EVIDENCE, weak]** An undergraduate case study reported more abdominal and rectus femoris activation in the bear walk than in the bird dog — [UMH thesis PDF](https://dspace.umh.es/jspui/bitstream/11000/25997/1/TFG%20-S%c3%a1nchez%20Egea%2c%20Juan.pdf)
- **[COACHING]** A review and practical guide to QMT exists (Kean University) — [Kean record](https://researchers.kean.edu/en/publications/quadrupedal-movement-training-a-brief-review-and-practical-guide/). Full text not accessed.
- **[COACHING]** Animal Flow's six components start with wrist mobilisations. Mike Fitch reports wrists were usually the first area to fatigue when he taught these moves. The Level 1 order is wrist mobilisations → Beast and Crab "activations" (static holds; two of the four base positions) → travelling Beast → Ape/Crab → Underswitch and other switches/transitions → flows — [Animal Flow, 6 components](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/understanding-the-6-components-of-animal-flow/); [AF Level 1 Manual 2023](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Animal-Flow-Level-1-Manual-English-2023.pdf). Fitch's guidance is to stop when form breaks down.
- **[EVIDENCE]** Turkish get-up: surface EMG of 8 glenohumeral muscle groups in 12 experienced practitioners (10 kettlebell trials each). Shoulder demand peaked at stage 2 (press to elbow support) and stage 5 (leg sweep) — St-Onge et al. 2019, [PubMed 30691756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30691756/). A registered trial comparing the TGU with stabilisation exercises (NCT05261789) has no results visible — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05261789). The view of the TGU as a shoulder/spine stability "lie detector" is coaching opinion — [StrongFirst](https://www.strongfirst.com/the-turkish-get-up-a-clinicians-perspective/).
- **[EVIDENCE]** Floor-transfer capacity predicts mortality. In 2,002 adults aged 51–80 (median follow-up about 6.3 years), each 1-point gain on the 0–10 sitting-rising test went with a 21% lower mortality. Scoring subtracts 1 point per hand or knee support and 0.5 per loss of stability — [Araújo et al. 2012, Eur J Prev Cardiol](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2047487312471759); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Sitting-rising_test). A 2025 follow-up of 4,282 adults aged 46–75 found scores of 8–10 linked to particularly low mortality risk — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/quick-not-easy-test-can-predict-long-live-study-suggests-rcna213626). This is association, not causation.

### Inferences
**[SYNTHESIS] Slot key:** W-Move = warm-up Move stage; W-Prime = warm-up Prime stage (specific, low fatigue); Pre-run = pre-run warm-up; CD = cooldown Move; Hold = cooldown Hold/PNF; Rest = rest-day flow; Snack = daily movement snack (2–5 min).
**Purpose key:** Wr = wrist prep; Sh = shoulder stability; Hip = hip mobility; Core = core/anti-rotation; Cond = conditioning; Coord = coordination.

**[SYNTHESIS] Catalogue.** The doses and level criteria are coaching conventions, not findings from sources.

| Movement | Primary purpose | Best slots | Warm-up dose | Progression L1 → L5 (pass criterion to advance) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wrist prep series** (quadruped wrist rocks, palms-up/down rocks, finger pulses, fist rolls) | Wr | W-Move before any hand-loaded drill; Snack | 30–60 s total, 5–10 reps per direction | L1 seated or table-top hands, partial load → L2 quadruped, small rocks → L3 full forward/back rocks → L4 pulses at end range → L5 rocks with knees lifted (beast-height). Pass: pain-free at 10 reps, no lingering ache the next day |
| **Quadruped rocking** (rock back to heels) | Hip (flexion), lumbopelvic control, Wr | W-Move (lower day); CD; Rest; Snack | 8–10 slow reps | L1 short range → L2 full range, neutral spine → L3 with breathing (exhale on rock-back) → L4 wide-knee/adductor rock → L5 rock with one knee lifted. Pass: reaches heels or the range limit without lumbar flexion "tuck" |

Pass: contralateral pattern, knees low, no hip sway |

## 2. Specific prep before squats, deadlifts, bench/OH press, pull-ups and running (and what to avoid pre-lift)

### Takeaway
The evidence supports specific, low-fatigue mobilisation and potentiation that resembles the session's main lift (RAMP's Mobilise/Potentiate logic). It warns that pre-fatiguing the core changes squat mechanics. Pre-lift crawling should therefore be short positional or skill exposure, and high-volume crawl conditioning belongs after lifting or on separate days. Before runs, dynamic movement is at least neutral and possibly positive for running economy and performance. Static stretching held for long durations has small negative effects on strength.

### Cited Findings
- **[GUIDELINE/COACHING]** RAMP (Ian Jeffreys), recommended by the UKSCA: Raise (temperature, HR, blood flow) → Activate & Mobilise (movement patterns and joints used in the session) → Potentiate (gradual increase of intensity toward session demands: sprints, jumps, bounds). Sessions should match warm-up content to session demands — [Human Kinetics, The RAMP System](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/strength-conditioning-fitness/the-ramp-system); [Science for Sport warm-ups PDF](https://www.scienceforsport.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/LM-Warm-Ups.pdf); [Jeffreys, *The Warm-Up* (2019)](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/en/publications/the-warm-up-maximise-performance-and-improve-long-term-athletic-d/)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Core fatigue changes squat mechanics. In 16 female bodybuilders, double-leg squats after a core-fatigue protocol showed significantly less knee flexion and more knee valgus, with no change in hip flexion. Single group, small sample — [Physical Treatments 2022 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/58d670ffd44048ab8b074bd06b54415d)
- **[EVIDENCE]** In squats at 75% 1RM, forward trunk inclination appeared greater under pre-fatigue than without it (single lab study, details unclear in the snippet). Whole-body fatigue (bodyweight squats to exhaustion, 21 male athletes) significantly altered squat kinematics — [PLOS ONE 2023 (PMC10681224)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10681224/). Trunk stabiliser EMG during squats to failure at 85% 1RM — [Clark et al. 2020, EJAP](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-020-04540-0) (results not visible)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Static stretching before exercise has a small negative effect on maximal strength that scales with total stretch duration. The smallest effects appear at ≤45 s per muscle, and stretching should not be the only warm-up activity — [Simic et al. 2013 meta-analysis (104 studies)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22316148/)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Running, mixed results:
  - In 8 recreational runners, warm-ups (10 min running + 5 min stretching) with static or dynamic stretching improved running economy and lowered perceived effort vs no stretching — [Int J Environ Res Public Health 2021 (PMC8391672)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8391672/)
  - In 7 well-trained runners, dynamic stretching improved endurance performance at 90% VO2max without changing running economy — [PubMed 25932984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25932984/)
  - Another trained-runner study found no effect on endurance performance — [PubMed 22266545](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22266545/)
  - A 2025 systematic review (15 studies, 181 participants; low certainty) found stretching did not significantly moderate running economy acutely, with no clear difference between stretching types — [Sports Medicine – Open 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-025-00859-0)
  - A review surfaced in the same search recommends dynamic stretching (total ≤220 s) over static, and static ≤60 s per muscle-tendon unit, when stretching without other warm-up — [PMC7857312 (attribution uncertain from snippet)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7857312)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Upper-body warm-ups were reviewed systematically for performance and injury — [PubMed 25694615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25694615/) (results not extracted)
- **[EVIDENCE]** TGU stages 2 and 5 produce peak shoulder EMG — [PubMed 30691756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30691756/)

### Inferences
- **[SYNTHESIS] Lift-specific prep menus**, each 2–4 min within W-Move/Prime:
  - **Squat:** quadruped rocking, deep squat hold/pry, cossack, spiderman lunge, beast hold 2 × 15 s (bracing rehearsal). Then empty-bar ramp sets, which serve as the true Potentiate.
  - **Deadlift:** inchworm (hamstrings), quadruped rocking with neutral spine, bear crawl 1 × 5 m (bracing), hollow hold 1 × 15 s. Avoid long hamstring static holds.
  - **Bench / overhead press:** wrist prep, crab reach (anterior shoulder and thoracic extension), lateral bear crawl or beast shoulder taps (serratus/scapular control), light half-TGU. Add a short wall-handstand line only for overhead days and only for eligible users.
  - **Pull-ups:** scapular pulls, active hang 2 × 10 s, hollow hold 2 × 15 s, crab reach. Keep passive dead hangs for the cooldown.
  - **Running:** spiderman/WGS, cossack, inchworm, short bear crawl forward/back, 2–3 frog hops or ape traverses as low-volume elastic prep. Then strides and drills.
- **[SYNTHESIS] Avoid before lifting:** timed crawl conditioning (e.g. 60 s+ continuous bear or lizard crawls, crawl "ladders"), high-rep frog hops, long hollow or plank holds (>30 s), passive hangs >30 s before pulling (grip fatigue) and lizard crawl before benching. Core-fatigue studies show changed squat mechanics after core pre-fatigue. Small studies, but they point consistently toward keeping pre-lift core work sub-fatiguing.
- **[SYNTHESIS] Running is more tolerant.** Moderate dynamic crawling and hopping is fine before easy runs. Before intervals or races, cut it to positional/elastic doses and finish with strides.

### Gaps
- No study tests animal-flow or crawling warm-ups against standard dynamic warm-ups for 1RM, bar speed or running performance.
- No data on how long the bracing "priming" effect of crawling lasts, or whether it transfers to loaded lifts.

## 3. Contraindications and modifications

### Takeaway
Most cautions here rest on clinical reasoning and coaching consensus, not trials. The firmer evidence:
- Head-down positions raise intraocular pressure. A 1985 JAMA study reported IOP more than doubling on inversion, so glaucoma or ocular hypertension means no handstands or inversions.
- Post-meniscus-repair protocols restrict deep loaded knee flexion for about 12 or more weeks.
- Pregnancy guidance flags fall risk and the supine position after the first trimester.
- Hang cautions centre on instability and hypermobility.
- Wrist issues are managed by reducing extension: fists, parallettes, wedges, elevation.

### Cited Findings
- **Wrist pain**
  - **[COACHING]** Parallettes or push-up bars keep the wrist neutral — [E3 Rehab](https://e3rehab.com/wrist-pain-rehab/). Fist/knuckle push-ups do the same — [Military.com](https://www.military.com/military-fitness/ask-stew/tactical-fitness-pushups-and-wrist-pain?amp). Towels under the heel of the hand reduce extension, and you may need to stop push-ups or elevate them while strengthening wrists — [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/wrist-pain-pushups); [Tonal](https://tonal.com/blogs/all/wrist-pain-during-pushups/)
  - **[COACHING]** For handstands, planche and similar, use parallettes, blocks or a straight-bar grip. Wrist push-up progressions start in push-up position and increase range as strength improves. Get out of pain first, then build strength. Sharp pain is never a good sign — [GMB wrists](https://gmb.io/wrists/); [Mike Reinold](https://mikereinold.com/wrist-pain-while-weight-bearing/)
  - **[COACHING]** Animal Flow places wrist mobilisation first because wrists were the first area to fatigue in new clients — [Animal Flow](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/understanding-the-6-components-of-animal-flow/)
- **Shoulder (hanging)**
  - **[EXPERT COMMENTARY]** Dead hangs need baseline strength, can stress passive tissue and, for some people, risk subluxation. They are not the best choice for shoulder hypermobility or instability — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.edu.au/what-are-dead-hangs-what-are-the-shoulder-pain-risks-and-how-do-i-do-them-safely-202346)
  - **[COMMERCIAL, unverified]** Claims that hangs "open the subacromial space" and remodel the acromion cite no research — [deadhangs site](https://deadhangs-com.pages.dev/shoulder-health)
  - **[EVIDENCE background]** Arm elevation narrows the subacromial space, and scapular orientation affects clearance — [Karduna 2005](https://pages.uoregon.edu/biomech/obl/articles/2005_karduna_jses.pdf). No study directly measuring hangs was found.
- **Knee / meniscus**
  - **[EVIDENCE]** High flexion angles load the posterior meniscus and may affect healing — [PMC8689273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689273). Loaded deep flexion may be safe after a period of restricted rehab — [PMC11698001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11698001). Meniscal root tears commonly present as medial knee pain worse with squatting (same search set).
  - **[GUIDELINE]** HSS post-op meniscus repair: avoid closed-chain end-range knee flexion (deep squat, lunge) in weeks 7–12; progressive squats >90° in weeks 13–20 — [HSS guideline PDF](https://www.hss.edu/files/HSSRehabilitationClinicalGuidelines-Knee-MeniscusRepair-Post-operative.pdf). The SLU protocol restricts loaded flexion >90° until week 8 — [SLU PDF](https://slu.edu/medicine/orthopaedic-surgery/sports-medicine/-pdf/meniscus-repair.pdf)
  - **[COACHING/clinical]** Non-surgical rehab uses low-load, long-duration flexion and progresses from bodyweight squats to split squats — [E3 Rehab meniscus](https://e3rehab.com/meniscus-tears/)
  - No source specifically on duck walks was found.
- **Inversions / hypertension / eyes**
  - **[EVIDENCE]** IOP rose within 1 minute in four head-down yoga poses and returned to baseline within 2 minutes of sitting. Downward dog produced the largest rise, with similar responses in glaucoma and non-glaucoma participants — [PLOS ONE 2015 (PMC4689525)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4689525); [AOA news](https://www.aoa.org/news/clinical-eye-care/head-down-yoga-poses-increase-eye-pressure-in-glaucoma-patients)
  - **[EVIDENCE]** IOP more than doubled on gravity inversion. The authors advised people with glaucoma or ocular hypertension to avoid inversion — [JAMA 1985](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/articlepdf/399933/jama_254_6_022.pdf)
  - **[EXPERT]** Glaucoma specialists advise avoiding headstands and head-down positions, and the long-term harm is uncertain — [Glaucoma NZ](https://glaucoma.org.nz/?p=3755); [Glaucoma Research Foundation](https://glaucoma.org/?p=6037)
  - No source on inversion in systemic hypertension was found.
- **Pregnancy**
  - **[GUIDELINE, secondary]** Avoid lying flat on the back in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters (reduced venous return through vena cava compression). Balance changes raise fall risk. ACOG advises against activities with increased fall or injury risk — [MotherToBaby](https://mothertobaby.org/fact-sheets/exercise-pregnancy/); [NCBI Bookshelf NBK430821](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430821/). The ACOG 2020 committee opinion itself was not accessed.
- **Older adults**
  - **[EVIDENCE]** Sitting-rising test score is inversely associated with mortality (21% lower per point) — [Araújo 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2047487312471759). That trial excluded people with serious mobility problems and severe joint pain — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/quick-not-easy-test-can-predict-long-live-study-suggests-rcna213626)
  - **[EVIDENCE]** The QMT RCT authors call for testing in older adults (not yet done) — [Animal Flow science page](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/science-behind-animal-flow/)

### Inferences
- **[SYNTHESIS] App rules (flag-driven substitutions):**
  - **Wrist flag:** wrist prep is mandatory. Offer a fists/parallettes/wedge toggle for all hand-loaded moves. Substitute forearm variants (forearm bear/plank). Remove handstand, lizard crawl and floreio switches until 2 pain-free sessions at the current level.
  - **Shoulder impingement flag:** keep scap pulls and active hang if pain-free; cap passive hang at 10–20 s with feet support; avoid wall handstand and deep crab reach if they reproduce pain; prefer bear and beast (below 90° flexion).
  - **Instability/hypermobility flag:** no passive hangs (active only), no crab-reach end range, slow TGU with light load.
  - **Knee/meniscus flag:** remove duck walk, frogger, ape and deep squat hold below the pain-free depth. Offer supported or box-height squat holds, half-range cossack and quadruped rocking with a limited range. Post-op: defer deep flexion per the surgeon's protocol (about 12+ weeks).
  - **Low back flag:** bias toward bird dog, beast hold, dead-bug-style hollow regressions and quadruped rocking within pain-free range. Avoid loaded crawls, long arch holds and fast floreio rotations until tolerated. (No sources found; clinical reasoning.)
  - **Pregnancy flag:** remove inversions (handstand), jumps (frogger, kangaroo), floreio switches and long supine holds (hollow) after trimester 1. Keep quadruped rocking, beast hold if comfortable, side-lying and supported squats, and cossack with support.
  - **Hypertension / glaucoma / eye flag:** no wall handstand or prolonged head-down positions. Shorten inchworm and downward-dog-type holds. For uncontrolled hypertension, avoid long breath-hold bracing.
  - **Older adult flag:** start with rolling patterns, quadruped rocking, supported squats and half-TGU (floor transfer practice). Avoid jumps until balance criteria are met. A sitting-rising self-test is a candidate benchmark.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence found on: duck-walk knee loads; hanging and subacromial space; inversion responses in people with systemic hypertension; low-back-pain-specific use of crawling.
- The original ACOG 2020/2023 text was not accessed.

## 4. How readiness/fatigue should change dosing

### Takeaway
No study tests readiness-adjusted animal-movement dosing. The general warm-up literature does show that excess intensity, excess duration and too little recovery impair performance. That supports a rule set: on low-readiness days, shift ground work to low-intensity, positional flows (an active-recovery character) and drop crawl conditioning and plyometric animal moves.

### Cited Findings
- **[EVIDENCE]** Warm-up intensity, duration and recovery can be traded off for similar gains. Too much intensity or too little recovery impairs short-term performance — [Bishop 2003, Warm up II](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12762825/)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Long warm-ups have been reported to cause fatigue (sprint mini-review) — [PMC10927743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10927743)
- **[EVIDENCE]** A 2025 meta-analysis (33 studies) found warm-ups did not clearly raise maximal strength but helped speed and power. The authors suggested poorly designed specific warm-ups may cause fatigue (via news summary) — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250911/Do-warm-ups-improve-muscle-strength.aspx)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Pre-fatigue of the core or whole body alters squat kinematics — [DOAJ 2022](https://doaj.org/article/58d670ffd44048ab8b074bd06b54415d); [PLOS ONE 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10681224/)
- **[COACHING]** Animal Flow: stop when form breaks down — [Animal Flow 6 components](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/understanding-the-6-components-of-animal-flow/)

### Inferences
- **[SYNTHESIS] Readiness tiers:**
  - **High:** full warm-up, including Prime-stage potentiation (frog hops, short bear crawls, beast taps). Crawl conditioning allowed as a post-lift finisher (e.g. 3–5 × 20 m bear crawl).
  - **Moderate:** standard warm-up. No conditioning finisher, or halve it.
  - **Low:** replace Prime-stage plyometric or animal moves with positional versions (beast hold instead of bear crawl; deep squat pry instead of frogger). Drop crawl conditioning entirely. Offer a 10–15 min low-intensity "recovery flow" option (quadruped rocking, rolling, crab reach, spiderman, slow underswitch) at RPE ≤3–4, nasal breathing.
  - **Very low / unwell:** snacks only (wrist prep, deep squat hold, active hang, 90/90).
- **[SYNTHESIS] Progression gating:** only advance a level when the pass criterion is met on two separate sessions at moderate-or-better readiness. Never advance on a low-readiness day.

### Gaps
- No studies on quadrupedal or ground flows as active recovery (HRV, soreness, next-day performance).
- No validated readiness thresholds for movement-practice dosing.

## 5. Time budget: minutes of ground/animal work in a warm-up without hurting lifting

### Takeaway
The evidence supports warm-ups that are brief (an aerobic portion under about 15 min plus a few specific efforts) and non-fatiguing, with static stretches ≤45–60 s per muscle. Within a typical 8–15 min RAMP warm-up, about 3–6 min of animal/ground movement in the Move/Prime stages is a reasonable, coaching-level budget. The exact thresholds have not been studied directly.

### Cited Findings
- **[EVIDENCE]** Warm-ups improved performance in 79% of criteria examined, with little evidence of harm — [Fradkin et al. 2010 meta-analysis (32 studies)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19996770/)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Better performance followed brief aerobic warm-ups (<15 min) plus a few activation sprints or similar efforts — [Sports Med 2015 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs40279-015-0376-x)
- **[EVIDENCE]** Static stretching's negative strength effect is smallest at ≤45 s per muscle — [Simic 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22316148/)
- **[EVIDENCE]** For running, use dynamic stretching (≤220 s total) or static stretching ≤60 s per muscle-tendon unit if stretching without other warm-up — [PMC7857312 (attribution uncertain)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7857312)
- **[GUIDELINE/COACHING]** RAMP structure: Raise → Activate/Mobilise → Potentiate, with intensity rising toward session demands — [Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/strength-conditioning-fitness/the-ramp-system)

### Inferences
- **[SYNTHESIS] Suggested budget per session type:**
  - **Lifting warm-up (8–12 min):** Raise 2–4 min (bike/row/skip) → Move 3–4 min (2–4 animal/ground drills from the lift-specific menu) → Prime 1–2 min (one low-fatigue animal drill + first ramp sets). Total animal work 3–6 min.
  - **Pre-run (6–10 min):** 3–5 min easy jog, 2–4 min dynamic/animal drills, then strides.
  - **Cooldown (5–10 min):** Roll 1–3 min → Move 2–3 min (crab reach, quadruped rocking, rolling) → Hold/PNF 2–4 min. Static holds of 30–60 s are fine here because performance is no longer at stake.
  - **Rest-day flow:** 10–20 min at low intensity.
  - **Daily snack:** 2–5 min, 1–3 drills.

### Gaps
- No study directly quantifies the "minutes of crawling before fatigue harms a 1RM or rep performance" threshold. The 2025 strength warm-up meta-analysis was available only via a news summary.
- Full texts of the RAMP chapter and the QMT papers could not be fetched (network restrictions), so the exact RAMP timing recommendations are unconfirmed.
