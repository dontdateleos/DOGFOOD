# Evidence on quadrupedal/animal-movement training, deep-squat resting, dead hangs and movement snacks

Method note: I read these sources through search-engine abstracts and snippets. Full-text fetches of sciencedirect.com, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and animalflow.com were blocked by this session's network allowlist. So I could not check exact tables, effect sizes or risk-of-bias details against full papers, and figures below are as reported in abstracts or summaries. Grades use this scale: **Strong** (several consistent RCTs or meta-analyses, or very large cohorts for associations), **Moderate** (one or two decent RCTs, or a meta-analysis with heterogeneity), **Weak** (single small trials, pilot or acute EMG work, cross-sectional data), **Expert opinion only** (practitioner, book or anecdote).

## 1. What do RCTs/controlled studies show for quadrupedal movement training (QMT) / Animal Flow?

### Takeaway
The QMT evidence base is tiny: about three controlled trials plus a handful of acute EMG and energy-cost studies, most of them by one research group (Buxton, Grove City College) and several hosted or promoted by the Animal Flow brand. The defensible claims are: QMT is moderate-intensity exercise (about 5.4 METs), it improved FMS scores and some hip/shoulder active ROM over 8 weeks in young adults, and one 4-week study in 22 students found better cognitive-flexibility test scores. QMT did **not** improve balance, grip strength or push-up endurance against control. No body-composition outcome has been tested, and no QMT RCT in older adults exists.

### Cited Findings
**Cognition and proprioception: Matthews et al. 2016**
- Matthews MJ, Yusuf M, Doyle C, Thompson C. "Quadrupedal movement training improves markers of cognition and joint repositioning." *Human Movement Science* 2016;47:70–80. DOI 10.1016/j.humov.2016.02.002. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167945716300185); [PubMed 26896559](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26896559/); [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning)
- Sample and design: n=22 physically active sports-science students (15 M, 7 F), split into a training group and a control group, with a 4-week progressive quadrupedal gait program. — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning)
- Results: the training group improved significantly on Wisconsin Card Sorting Task perseverative errors, non-perseverative errors and conceptual-level responses, and the control group did not. Joint reposition sense improved **only at 20° of shoulder flexion**, the angle the training targeted. — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/294874826_Quadrupedal_movement_training_improves_markers_of_cognition_and_joint_repositioning)
- Limitations: the per-group effect sizes were not available in the retrieved text. The WCST has known practice effects, and the control group did no alternative novel motor task, so novelty or learning effects cannot be separated from anything specific to quadrupedal movement (my inference, see Inferences). — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning)
- Name collision: "QMT" also stands for **Quadrato Motor Training**, a different standing step-pattern intervention with its own cognitive-flexibility literature. Do not cite those studies as quadrupedal evidence. — [PMC4511076 (Quadrato)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4511076/)

**Mobility, FMS, strength and balance: Buxton et al. 2022 (JSCR)**
- Buxton JD, Prins PJ, Miller MG, et al. "The Effects of a Novel Quadrupedal Movement Training Program on Functional Movement, Range of Motion, Muscular Strength, and Endurance." *J Strength Cond Res* 2022;36(8):2186–2193 (online 2020). DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003818. — [PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/); [Ovid full text](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/fulltext/10.1519/jsc.0000000000003818~the-effects-of-a-novel-quadrupedal-movement-training-program); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345976136_The_Effects_of_a_Novel_Quadrupedal_Movement_Training_Program_on_Functional_Movement_Range_of_Motion_Muscular_Strength_and_Endurance)
- Design: 42 college-age adults randomized to Animal Flow-based QMT or control, with 60-min classes 2×/week for 8 weeks on top of usual activity. — [Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/nsca-jscr/fulltext/10.1519/jsc.0000000000003818~the-effects-of-a-novel-quadrupedal-movement-training-program); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/)
- FMS composite: the QMT group gained 1.62 ± 1.53 points versus 0.33 ± 1.15 in control (p=0.004). Advanced-movement and fundamental-stability subscores also improved more (p=0.002 for advanced movements). A rough Cohen's d computed from these means and SDs is about 0.9–1.0. That figure was calculated by the search tool, not reported by the paper. — [PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/)
- Active ROM improved more than control on **7 of 18** measures, mostly at the hips and shoulders. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345976136_The_Effects_of_a_Novel_Quadrupedal_Movement_Training_Program_on_Functional_Movement_Range_of_Motion_Muscular_Strength_and_Endurance)
- **Null results:** no between-group differences in dynamic balance (Y-Balance), handgrip strength or push-up endurance. Core endurance was not measured. — [PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/)
- The Grove City College press release confirms the publication. — [GCC](https://www.gcc.edu/Home/Academics/Faculty-Directory/Faculty-Detail/journal-to-publish-buxton-study-of-quadrupedal-movement-training)

**Energy cost: Buxton et al. 2022 (Frontiers)**
- "A comparison of the energy demands of quadrupedal movement training to walking." *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* 2022. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2022.992687. n=30. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.992687)
- A 60-min beginner Animal Flow class averaged 6.7 ± 1.8 kcal/min, 5.4 ± 1.0 METs and HR 127 ± 16 bpm. Self-paced walking averaged 4.3 METs and HR 100. HR-matched walking averaged 6.1 METs, HR 125 and 7.6 kcal/min, so it burned **more** energy than QMT at the same heart rate. The "Flow" segment peaked at 7.0 ± 1.7 METs and HR 153. Males showed higher EE and METs. The authors conclude that QMT meets the ACSM moderate-intensity criteria. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.992687); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/3e6b34e3beab4712b86c63dc05e8ebf5)

**Athletes: Animal Flow as a supplement in basketball (12-week RCT)**
- Savaş et al., *Pamukkale Journal of Sport Sciences*, Dec 2025. University basketball players added Animal Flow 2×/week after the warm-up. — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/psbd/article/1672553); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397273892_Animal_Flow_as_a_Supplementary_Training_Tool_for_Basketball_A_12-Week_Randomized_Controlled_Trial)
- The abstract reports significant group × time interactions favouring Animal Flow for agility, core endurance, proprioception, repeated-sprint ability and flexibility. One snippet, however, says the **between-group** analysis was significant only for functional movement (p<0.05). The two statements conflict, so verify the full text before citing. The sample size was not retrieved. — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/psbd/article/1672553)

**EMG and acute studies (crawling and bear crawl)**
- Trunk EMG (Pyka et al., "Effects of Static, Stationary, and Traveling Trunk Exercises on Muscle Activation"): activation of every trunk muscle tested was higher in the bear limb-lift and the travelling bear crawl than in the static bear hold. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318471304_Effects_of_Static_Stationary_and_Traveling_Trunk_Exercises_on_Muscle_Activation)
- Shoulder EMG ("Activity of Different Shoulder Muscles during Dynamic Bear Crawl Exercise," *Asian Pacific Journal of Health Sciences*): lower- and middle-trapezius activity was significantly **greater in the push-up plus** than in the dynamic bear crawl. The bear crawl is not a superior scapular-stabilizer exercise. — [APJHS PDF](https://apjhs.com/index.php/apjhs/article/download/1541/1185); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358692914_Activity_of_Different_Shoulder_Muscles_during_Dynamic_Bear_Crawl_Exercise)
- Review: adult crawling EMG studies show substantial activity in trunk stabilizers, shoulders, triceps, quadriceps, calves and hamstrings ("Quadrupedal Movement Training: A Brief Review and Practical Guide"). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371807338_Quadrupedal_Movement_Training_A_Brief_Review_and_Practical_Guide)
- Hands-and-knees crawling sEMG of 30 muscles in 10 healthy adults characterized inter-limb coordination and muscle synergies. This is biomechanics research with no training outcome. — [PMC10235503](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10235503/); [PMC10972899](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10972899/)
- A newer paper titled "Comparison of muscle activation during quadrupedal movement training and traditional bodyweight exercises" exists, but its findings were not retrievable. — [BVB record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_3133417957)

**Brand and practitioner material**
- Animal Flow's "Science" page and manuals list the studies above and say that research on complex adult quadrupedal movement is "a newer field." The studies listed are mostly the same Buxton/Matthews papers. — [Animal Flow science page](https://animalflow.com/science_of_animalflow/); [AF L1 manual](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/AF-Manual-L1-A4-Eng-2021-Student-Download.pdf)
- Primal Play and similar sites make broad claims (strength, coordination, "brain" benefits) without new trial data. — [Primal Play](https://www.primalplay.com/blog/the-physical-and-mental-benefits-of-crawling)

### Inferences
- Claims an app can honestly make: "moderate-intensity whole-body exercise," "may improve movement-screen scores and some hip/shoulder mobility over about 8 weeks (one RCT)," and "early evidence (one small trial) of improved cognitive-flexibility test scores." The app should avoid "builds strength," "improves balance," "burns more than walking," "rewires the brain" and any body-composition claim.
- The Matthews effect may come from learning a novel, complex motor skill rather than from quadrupedalism as such, because the control group did nothing comparable.
- Dosing that matches the trials: 2×/week, 60 min for 8 weeks (Buxton), progressive 4-week gait practice (Matthews), or about 15 min added after a warm-up 2×/week (basketball RCT; exact duration not retrieved).
- The heavy overlap between the research group and the brand, plus small samples, means publication and allegiance bias cannot be excluded.

### Gaps
- No QMT or Animal Flow RCT in older adults was found, and none for body composition.
- Only one adolescent or child study surfaced: a quasi-experimental (non-randomized) bodyweight program including quadrupedal exercises in 8–10-year-olds. I could not retrieve its citation.
- No systematic review or meta-analysis of QMT exists, as far as these searches show.
- Effect sizes and confidence intervals for Matthews 2016 were not retrievable.
- Crawling in rehabilitation (e.g., neurological or pediatric "crawling therapy" for adults) has only blog-level sources, such as [NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/crawling-therapy-for-adults/). No controlled trials were found.

## 2. Deep/resting squat, hip/ankle mobility and habitual-squatting populations (including harms)

### Takeaway
No RCT shows that deep-squat "resting" improves hip or ankle mobility or joint health. The best population data, from the Beijing Osteoarthritis Study, links **prolonged daily squatting** (about 2 h/day or more) to roughly **2× higher odds of tibiofemoral knee OA**. Occupational meta-analyses agree: squatting OR about 1.9 for knee OA. The same Beijing cohort has very low hip OA prevalence (80–90% below US whites), but squatting has **not** been shown to explain it. Floor sit-to-rise ability is associated with lower mortality, which is observational evidence only.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang Y, Hunter DJ, Nevitt MC, et al. "Association of squatting with increased prevalence of radiographic tibiofemoral knee osteoarthritis: the Beijing Osteoarthritis Study." *Arthritis & Rheumatism* 2004. Cross-sectional, random sample of Beijing residents aged 60 and over. Conclusion: prolonged squatting is a strong risk factor for tibiofemoral OA and explains a substantial share of the higher knee-OA prevalence in Beijing compared with Framingham whites. — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/a91d0d4277e2257d710b2c43d9146f84); [Oxford NDORMS listing](https://www.ndorms.ox.ac.uk/publications/125932)
- Dose-response from the same data (reported in a Chinese-language analysis): for squatting at age 25 versus under 30 min/day, adjusted ORs were 1.1, 1.0, 1.7 and **2.0** for ≥120 min/day in men, and 1.4, 1.3, 1.2 and **2.4** in women. Trend p=0.074 (men) and p=0.077 (women). — [BVS / Chinese J Epidemiol](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/ar/wpr-232325)
- Conflict: a secondary magazine summary describes the top male category as over 3 h/day, with about 70% higher risk at 2–3 h. Category cut-points need checking in the original. — [Ironman Magazine](https://www.ironmanmagazine.com/?p=17188)
- Occupational meta-analysis (16 studies, Osteoarthritis & Cartilage 2011, "Occupational risk factors for osteoarthritis of the knee: a meta-analysis"): squatting OR 1.9, kneeling OR 1.7, heavy lifting OR 1.6 for knee OA. — [OARSI PDF](https://www.oarsijournal.com/article/S1063-4584(11)00067-7/pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/50303663_Occupational_risk_factors_for_osteoarthritis_of_the_knee_A_meta-analysis)
- An updated occupational systematic review (Osteoarthritis & Cartilage 2020/21) found higher knee-OA risk in males, industry-based studies and studies measuring lifetime exposure. — [OARSI 2020 review](https://www.oarsijournal.com/article/S1063-4584(20)30724-X/fulltext)
- A German case-control study of individual and occupational knee-OA risk factors also addresses kneeling and squatting exposure. — [PMC2911872](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2911872/)
- Nevitt MC et al. "Very low prevalence of hip osteoarthritis among Chinese elderly in Beijing, China, compared with whites in the United States." *Arthritis & Rheumatism* 2002. Crude radiographic hip-OA prevalence was 0.9% (women) and 1.1% (men) at ages 60–89, against about 4.1% in US whites aged 60–74, and it did not rise with age. The grant planned to test historical squatting as a risk factor, but no squatting-protects-hips result was found. Hip morphology differences have been proposed as an alternative explanation. — [GHDx record](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/114971); [NIH grant R01 AR043804](https://grantome.com/grant/NIH/R01-AR043804-02); [PMC3178680](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3178680)
- Squat depth determinants (lab study): in men, ankle dorsiflexion with a flexed knee and hip-flexion ROM predicted squat depth. In women, dorsiflexion with an extended knee and dorsiflexor strength did. — [PMC4415844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4415844)
- Restricting dorsiflexion with a wedge reduced peak knee flexion and increased medial knee displacement (valgus) in squatting. — [PubMed 22100617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22100617/)
- Once hip flexion is exhausted, the pelvis tilts posteriorly ("butt wink") as a compensation. — [PMC8439674](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8439674)
- Floor sit-to-rise test: Brito LBB et al. *Eur J Prev Cardiol* 2012. n=2,002 adults aged 51–80, median follow-up 6.3 y, 159 deaths. Each 1-point higher score (0–10) was associated with about 21% lower mortality, adjusted for age, sex and BMI. — [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2047487312471759); [ESC press release](https://escardio.org/The-ESC/Press-Office/Press-releases/Ability-to-sit-and-rise-from-the-floor-is-closely-correlated-with-all-cause-mort)

### Inferences
- The harm signal is about **hours per day of habitual squatting** (occupational or lifestyle), not minutes. Brief daily deep-squat holds of a few minutes sit far below the exposure linked to knee OA. The app should still keep squat-rest holds short, give people with knee OA, meniscal pathology or knee pain an alternative (supported or elevated-heel squat), and avoid claiming it "protects your joints."
- "Squatting cultures have healthier hips" is not established. The low hip-OA prevalence in Beijing is real, but its cause is unknown and morphology or genetics are plausible.
- Ankle dorsiflexion is the usual limiter of a heels-down deep squat. Heel elevation is an honest regression.
- The sit-to-rise data support practising floor transitions as a functional skill, but they are observational. The app must not imply that practising the test adds years of life.

### Gaps
- No RCT was found testing daily deep-squat resting on hip or ankle ROM, back pain or joint outcomes.
- I could not verify Hemmerich et al. (2006, Clinical Biomechanics) kinematic norms for deep squatting and kneeling in Indian populations, so I give no specific degree requirements here.
- Exact cut-points and sample size from Zhang 2004 were not retrievable (blocked full text).
- No longitudinal data separating lifestyle squatting from heavy occupational load in modern populations were found.

## 3. Dead hangs / passive hanging: shoulder health, subacromial space, impingement, grip

### Takeaway
I found **no peer-reviewed controlled trial or imaging study** showing that passive hanging widens the subacromial space, reshapes the acromion or treats impingement or rotator-cuff disease. The Kirsch hanging protocol rests on a self-published book and an unpublished case series (expert opinion only). Grip strength is a robust **prognostic marker** of mortality and CVD, but it is a marker, not evidence that training grip by hanging lowers risk.

### Cited Findings
- Kirsch's claims: hanging stretches the coracoacromial ligament, remodels the acromion to enlarge the subacromial space, and cures about 99% of his impingement patients without acromioplasty. Reviewers note that no study substantiates this. — [APTEI review](https://www.aptei.ca/library-article/single-exercise-to-cure-rotator-cuff-impingement-syndrome/); [Benchk summary](https://benchk.us/journal/kirsch-hanging-protocol)
- The 92-patient Kirsch series (including 16 MRI-confirmed tears) was never peer-reviewed. — [Benchk summary](https://benchk.us/journal/kirsch-hanging-protocol)
- A clinic review rates "hanging relieves impingement or frozen shoulder" as expert opinion, case series or anecdote, and unproven. It notes that no RCT compares hanging with usual physiotherapy, no imaging shows acromial change, and hanging in the painful early phase of frozen shoulder can aggravate the capsule. — [Acibadem blog](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/dead-hangs-what-hanging-from-a-bar-does-for-shoulders-grip-and-spine-and-who-should-avoid-it/)
- Unverifiable figures circulating online (e.g., "hanging increases subacromial space by 1–2 mm," and a "2019 J Shoulder Elbow Surg" study on passive versus active hangs) could not be traced to primary papers. Treat them as unsupported. — [Gripp blog](https://www.gripp.fit/blog/dead-hangs-for-shoulder-pain-what-the-research-actually-says); [deadhangs.com](https://deadhangs.com/shoulder-health/)
- Anatomical plausibility only: a cadaver study (Yamamoto et al. 2010) found the humeral head contacts and stretches the coracoacromial ligament in flexion and abduction. This is reported secondhand. — [Benchk summary](https://benchk.us/journal/kirsch-hanging-protocol)
- Related trial context: the PASE trial protocol (pain-allowed versus pain-avoided rotator-cuff rehab) shows that whether to train into pain is still unsettled. That bears on Kirsch's advice to hang through pain. — [PMC10880378](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10880378/)
- Grip strength as a health marker, PURE (Leong DP et al. *Lancet* 2015): per 5-kg lower grip, HR 1.16 (1.13–1.20) for all-cause mortality, 1.17 (1.11–1.24) for CV mortality and 1.07 (1.02–1.11) for MI. Grip was a stronger predictor of all-cause and CV mortality than systolic BP. The authors say trials are needed to test whether improving strength lowers risk. — [PubMed 25982160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/25982160); [NEJM CardioExchange](https://blogs.nejm.org/cardioexchange/?p=48178)
- Meta-analysis of prospective cohorts (2017), per 5-kg lower grip: HR 1.16 (1.12–1.20) all-cause, 1.21 (1.14–1.29) CVD, 1.09 (1.05–1.14) stroke and 1.07 (1.03–1.11) CHD. — [PubMed 28549705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/28549705)
- Adults aged 60 and over (systematic review and meta-analysis): low versus high grip HR 1.79 (1.26–2.55) for mortality on categorical analyses. — [PubMed 26016893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26016893/)
- Objectively measured physical capability (grip, walking speed, chair rises, standing balance) predicts mortality (Cooper et al., BMJ 2010 meta-analysis). — [PubMed 20829298](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20829298/)

### Inferences
- Honest framing: "Hanging is a simple way to train grip endurance and load the shoulders overhead; grip strength is associated with long-term health." Avoid "decompresses your spine or shoulders," "widens the subacromial space," "fixes impingement" and "avoids surgery."
- A sensible dose (practitioner-level, not trial-derived): start with feet-supported or partial-bodyweight hangs, build to 3–5 accumulated sets of about 10–30 s, scaled to bodyweight and grip. Exclude or modify for acute shoulder pain, frozen shoulder (early phase), recent dislocation or instability, and post-surgical shoulders.
- Grip is only one marker. The PURE association does not mean grip-specific training lowers mortality.

### Gaps
- No RCT, controlled trial or ultrasound/MRI study of hanging on subacromial space or impingement outcomes was found. My searches turned up only blogs and the Kirsch book, and PubMed could not be fetched directly to confirm absence.
- No trial was found measuring whether hang training improves grip strength more than other grip work.
- No injury-rate data for hanging was found.
- The PURE sample size was not shown in retrieved snippets (it is commonly cited as about 140,000 across 17 countries, but I did not verify this here).

## 4. Movement variability / "movement snacks" / exercise snacking through the day

### Takeaway
"Exercise snacks" (brief bouts, usually ≤5 min, several times a day) have **moderate-certainty** evidence for improving cardiorespiratory fitness in inactive adults. In the BJSM 2025 review the effect was large but heterogeneous (g≈1.37), and evidence for strength, body composition and cardiometabolic markers is null or weak. The idea that **mobility or "movement-variety" snacks** (crawling, squat holds, hangs) improve health has no direct trial evidence. The evidence covers vigorous snacks such as stair climbing and bodyweight intervals.

### Cited Findings
- Rodríguez et al. *British Journal of Sports Medicine* 2025, "Effect of exercise snacks on fitness and cardiometabolic health in physically inactive individuals: systematic review and meta-analysis." DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2025-110027 (online 7 Oct 2025). 11 RCTs, n=414 (69% women), ages about 19–74. — [BMJ Group release](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/); [Univ. Birmingham record](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/effect-of-exercise-snacks-on-fitness-and-cardiometabolic-health-i/); [BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/2025/10/24/exercise-snacks-small-bouts-big-benefits/)
  - Definition: structured bouts of ≤5 min, at least 2×/day, ≥3 days/week, for ≥2 weeks. HealthDay misreports this as "≥5 min." — [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/); [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/general-health/brief-bouts-of-exercise-improve-cardiorespiratory-fitness)
  - Cardiorespiratory fitness in adults: g=1.37 (95% CI 0.58–2.17), k=6, I²=71.4%, moderate certainty. — [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/)
  - Muscular endurance in older adults: g=0.40 (0.06–0.75), k=4, very low certainty. — [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/)
  - No significant effects on lower-limb strength, body composition, blood pressure or lipids. Compliance was 91.1% and adherence 82.8%. — [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/)
- Chen et al. *Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine* 2025: 27 studies, n=970, VO2max SMD 0.63 (0.45–0.82). — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcvm.2025.1643153/full)
- Wan et al. (2025), on exercise snacks, cardiometabolic health and body composition: 12 RCTs plus 2 non-RCTs, n=483. Its numeric conclusions were not retrieved. — [PMC12354995](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12354995/); [HKBU record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/effects-of-exercise-snacks-on-cardiometabolic-health-and-body-com/)
- An age-stratified meta-analysis found VO2max gains in younger and middle-aged groups but **no significant effect in the oldest stratum**. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1279770726001739)
- A physical-function meta-analysis (11 RCTs) reported higher VO2max and Wpeak in adults and better lower-limb function in older adults. — [PMC13091112](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091112/)
- Later coverage of a review in sedentary adults: the VO2max improvement lost statistical significance after adjusting for publication bias. — [News-Medical, Oct 2026](https://news-medical.net/news/20261006/Short-bursts-of-exercise-may-improve-fitness-and-blood-sugar-in-sedentary-adults.aspx)

### Inferences
- An app can claim "short, frequent bouts can improve fitness in inactive people" (moderate evidence), but only for vigorous snacks (stairs, fast bodyweight intervals). For an already-trained runner or lifter, the fitness benefit is likely small and untested.
- Low-intensity mobility snacks (squat rest, hang, crawl for 30–60 s) can be framed honestly as breaking up sitting and practising skills. Do not promise fitness or metabolic gains from them.
- High adherence (about 83–91%) is the strongest practical argument for snack formats.

### Gaps
- No trial was found testing "movement variability" or varied-movement snacks (crawl, squat, hang) as a distinct intervention.
- Effect sizes are heterogeneous and the trials are small. Publication bias is flagged in at least one review.

## 5. Effects in older adults; safety and injury data (wrist and shoulder loading)

### Takeaway
There is **no QMT or crawling trial in older adults** and **no injury-surveillance data** for crawling, hanging or squat-resting. Safety guidance is practitioner-level: progressive wrist loading, surface choice and fist or knee-supported modifications. General exercise in older adults reduces fall-related injuries and has a very low adverse-event rate, but that evidence does not cover quadrupedal work specifically.

### Cited Findings
- I found no peer-reviewed study of crawling and wrist injury, and no QMT trial in older adults. Commentary notes that adult quadrupedal research is small and that crawling places significant demands on the wrists, shoulders and knees. — [Having Good Health (Harvard-linked summary)](https://havinggoodhealth.substack.com/p/harvard-scientists-are-rethinking); [Biology Insights](https://biologyinsights.com/is-quadrobics-good-for-you-benefits-and-risks/)
- Practitioner risk factors: forced wrist extension on flat palms, hard surfaces, insufficient shoulder mobility and skipping progression. Suggested mitigations are wrist prep, gradual loading, mats or turf, fist or parallette modifications, and seeing a physio for people with existing joint injuries. — [Biology Insights](https://biologyinsights.com/is-quadrobics-good-for-you-benefits-and-risks/); [Fitness Warrior Nation](https://www.fitnesswarriornation.com/quadrobics-exercise-trend/)
- Exercise for older adults (meta-analysis of RCTs) significantly reduced fall-related injuries with a very low exercise-related injury rate. This covers exercise generally, not crawling. — [PMC6892137](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6892137/)
- Exercise snacks in the oldest stratum showed no significant VO2max effect, though older adults did improve muscular endurance and lower-limb function. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1279770726001739); [BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/); [PMC13091112](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13091112/)
- Floor sit-to-rise ability in people aged 51–80 is associated with mortality, which supports floor-transition practice as functionally relevant. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2047487312471759)
- Hanging in painful early frozen shoulder may aggravate the capsule (clinic opinion). — [Acibadem blog](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/dead-hangs-what-hanging-from-a-bar-does-for-shoulders-grip-and-spine-and-who-should-avoid-it/)
- Knee-OA risk rises with prolonged daily squatting in people aged 60 and over in Beijing. — [Scilit (Zhang 2004)](https://scilit.com/publications/a91d0d4277e2257d710b2c43d9146f84)

### Inferences
- For older users: put knee-supported quadruped positions (bird-dog, hands-and-knees rocking) and floor-transition practice before bear crawls. Offer fist or handle options for the wrists, supported or partial-bodyweight hangs, and elevated or supported squats. Screen for wrist pain, shoulder pain or instability, knee OA, osteoporosis (fall risk getting to the floor) and blood-pressure issues with inverted or loaded positions.
- Progression logic (practitioner-derived): static holds first (bear hold), then limb lifts, then short travelling crawls, then flows. The EMG data show this sequence also raises trunk demand.

### Gaps
- There is no injury incidence for QMT, Animal Flow, crawling, hanging or deep-squat resting in any population.
- No wrist-load (force-plate) quantification of crawling was found, and no studies in people with osteoporosis, hypertension or joint replacement.

## 6. Claim-by-claim evidence grades (effect size, n, quality)

Only exercise snacks (for cardiorespiratory fitness) and grip strength (as a prognostic marker) reach moderate-to-strong evidence. Almost every QMT, crawling, squat-rest and hanging benefit is weak or expert-opinion only. The best-supported **harm** is prolonged daily squatting and knee OA (moderate, observational).

### Inferences
- A copy-safe claim set for the app: "moderate-intensity full-body movement," "can improve movement quality and some hip and shoulder mobility (small trials)," "practises getting down to and up from the floor, a skill linked to healthy ageing," "trains grip, a strong marker of health," and "short movement breaks can build fitness if they are vigorous."
- Claims the app should not make: brain rewiring or neuroplasticity, spinal or shoulder decompression, curing impingement, fat loss, balance improvement, or "squatting cultures don't get arthritis."
- Trial-matched dosing for QMT: 2×/week, 20–60 min, 4–12 weeks. For snacks, ≤5 min, ≥2×/day, ≥3 days/week. For hangs and squat holds, no trial-based dose exists, so present them as practitioner guidance.

### Gaps
- Nearly all QMT studies come from a small set of authors with links to the commercial system. Independent replication is lacking.
- Full-text verification of effect sizes and risk of bias was blocked (network allowlist). Before quoting exact numbers in-app, confirm them against the original PDFs: Matthews 2016, Buxton 2022, Savaş 2025, Zhang 2004 and Rodríguez 2025.
