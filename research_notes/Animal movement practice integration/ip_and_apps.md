Not legal advice. Research current to Nov 2026. A methods note matters for how much weight to put on the trademark data: in this session the USPTO TSDR, Justia trademark pages, animalflow.com and gmb.io could not be fetched directly (blocked by network allowlist). ## Trademarks: which movement-system names are registered, by whom, and for what

### Takeaway
The system *names* are the protected assets: ANIMAL FLOW (Class 41, fitness services, registered 2013, Sec. 8 & 15 accepted 2018), MOVNAT (Classes 41 and 25), GINASTICA NATURAL (fitness instruction, renewed 2017), ORIGINAL STRENGTH (Class 41), GMB / GMB FITNESS (online fitness services, app filings), and IDOPORTAL MOVEMENT CULTURE (a monkey-design mark for **clothing only**, Class 25). I found no registration for the generic locomotion terms (bear crawl, crab walk, ape, beast) or for "Primal Movement Chains". So DOGFOOD can teach the movements under generic names but should not use the system names to label or market its content.

### Inferences
- The Ido Portal registration covers only apparel, so it gives little basis for a claim over movement content. A Class 25 registration does not cover app or fitness services. The bigger risks are (a) using the name "Ido Portal" or "Movement Culture" as a label, which raises false-endorsement and right-of-publicity issues (next section), and (b) reusing the monkey logo. Also, the registration date was 2015-09-15, so a Section 8 declaration was due 2020-09 to 2021-09 (grace period to about 2022-03). The "700 Registered" status in the snippet dates from 2015 and may be stale. The registration could have been cancelled for non-filing. Check TSDR.
- ANIMAL FLOW is incontestable for fitness training and classes. A DOGFOOD session titled "Animal Flow" or "Animal Flow-style" would likely be confusing use in the same Class 41 space. "Inspired by Animal Flow" in marketing is also risky (see Animal Flow's own rules below).
- Generic anatomical and animal names are low risk: bear crawl, crab walk, lizard/spider crawl, duck walk, frog jump, inchworm, gorilla shuffle, deep squat. "Beast", "Ape", "Crab" are not registered as marks, but as a *set* ("ABCs", "Loaded Beast", "Underswitch", "Scorpion Reach", "Side Kickthrough") they signal Animal Flow. The safer course is DOGFOOD's own descriptive names (e.g. "quadruped hover" or "bear hold" instead of "Loaded Beast"; "bear-to-crab switch" instead of "Underswitch").
- Avoid "Elements" with the Bear/Monkey/Frogger/Crab set, "Pressing Reset" (filed by Original Strength Systems), "MovNat" and "Ginastica Natural". "Natural movement" in lower case is a common descriptive phrase. Whether MovNat has registered it was not checked (see Gaps).

- MovNat's current owner of record. Whether "NATURAL MOVEMENT" or "MOVNAT" variants are registered for apps (Class 9).
- No US record for "Primal Movement Chains" found. It may be unregistered, or indexed differently.

## Copyright vs. exercise: what can and cannot be protected

### Takeaway
In the US, exercises, poses and their sequences are not copyrightable. The Ninth Circuit held this in *Bikram* (2015), and the Copyright Office's 2012 Statement of Policy says the same. What is protected is the *expression*: videos, photos, illustrations, written instructions and program text, logos, music, and in rare cases true choreography. DOGFOOD can include the same movements and even similar progressions, but must film, write and illustrate its own content.

### Cited Findings
- *Bikram's Yoga College of India, L.P. v. Evolation Yoga, LLC*, 803 F.3d 1032 (9th Cir. Oct. 8, 2015). Bikram's sequence of 26 poses and 2 breathing exercises is not copyrightable. It is an idea, process or system under 17 U.S.C. §102(b), it is not protectable as a compilation, and it is not a choreographic work. — [FindLaw opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1715117.html); [Stanford Fair Use summary](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/case/bikrams-yoga-college-v-evolation-yoga); [Fried Frank PDF of opinion](https://fkks.com/uploads/news/Bikrams_Yoga_College_of_India_LP_v_Evolation_Yoga_LLC.pdf)
- The court's reasoning is that copyright protects the book describing the sequence, not a monopoly on practicing the activity it describes. — [Wikipedia: Copyright claims on Bikram Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_claims_on_Bikram_Yoga); [Nova copyright blog](https://copyright.nova.edu/yoga-poses/)
- The Copyright Office's Statement of Policy, 77 Fed. Reg. 37605 (June 22, 2012), says "Exercise is not a category of authorship in section 102 and thus a compilation of exercises would not be copyrightable subject matter." It treats selection and arrangement of exercises as unregistrable and says earlier "compilation of exercises" registrations were issued in error. — [Federal Register (govinfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2012-06-22/html/2012-15235.htm); [copyright.gov PDF](https://copyright.gov/fedreg/2012/77fr37605.pdf); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/copyright/187742/no-copyright-protection-for-yoga-poses-and-sequence-of-poses); [JD Supra (Komen)](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/a-copyright-office-double-header-16970)
- The same statement separates copyrightable choreography from "yoga or exercise positions or other physical movements". Simple exercises and poses do not meet the choreographic-work standard of "an integrated, coherent, and expressive whole." — [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2012-06-26-one-less-copyright-issue-worry-about-gym); [UNC JOLT](https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/?p=1057); [Copyright Office NewsNet 461](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2012/461.html)
- Law360 headline: "Exercise Routines Still Not Protectable, Copyright Office Says". — [Law360](https://law360.com/ip/articles/352649/exercise-routines-still-not-protectable-copyright-office-says)
- Fitness disputes now tend to be fought through other claims. Tracy Anderson Mind and Body sued former trainer Megan Roup over alleged reuse of choreography-based routines; the case turned on contract and statements, not a clean exercise-copyright win. — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/?p=83006) (via search snippet; not fully verified)
- Animal Flow distributes copyrighted manuals: a Level 1 Workshop Student Manual (2023) and a Level 2 manual. — [AF L1 Manual PDF](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Animal-Flow-Level-1-Manual-English-2023.pdf); [AF L2 Manual PDF](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/AF-Manual-L2-A4-English-Student-Download.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Safe:** teaching a bear crawl, crab reach or "ape"-style lateral squat-hop, and building your own levels, dose (sets, seconds, meters) and progressions. *Bikram* means even a progression closely resembling another system's is not copyright infringement as such.
- **Infringing or high-risk:**
  - Embedding or re-hosting another system's videos or GIFs, or tracing their thumbnails.
  - Copying or closely paraphrasing manual text, cue scripts or coaching language, including the Animal Flow L1/L2 manuals.
  - Reproducing their illustrations or photos.
  - Using their logos or music.
  - Reproducing a named, signature flow *with its branding and presentation* ("the [System] Level 1 Flow"). Here the risk comes from trademark and contract, not copyright in the sequence.
- Contract can restrict what copyright doesn't. Anyone on the DOGFOOD team who attended an AF, MovNat, GMB or Original Strength course or bought a program may be bound by the workshop, terms-of-service or certification terms. Check those before reusing course material, even in paraphrase.
- Jurisdiction: *Bikram* binds the Ninth Circuit, and the Copyright Office policy is national. Other countries (EU, UK, India) have their own law. The App Store is global, but this is low practical risk for generic movements filmed in-house.

### Gaps
- The current text of Compendium (Third) §805 and §313.4 on exercise and choreography was not retrieved this session.
- The rule that names, titles and short phrases (e.g. "Loaded Beast") are not copyrightable (37 C.F.R. §202.1(a); Copyright Office Circular 33) is my background knowledge, not verified here. Confirm on copyright.gov.
- No case found involving copyright in an *app's* exercise sequence specifically.

## Safe naming practice: generic vs. branded terms, personal names, App Store 5.2

### Takeaway
Use generic, descriptive movement names. Don't use system names or people's names to label content. If you mention a system or person, do it only factually, sparingly, without logos, and with no implied affiliation. Apple's guideline 5.2.1 explicitly bars using third-party trademarks without permission and "copycat" names or metadata, which includes the app name, subtitle, keywords and screenshots.

### Cited Findings
- Guideline 5.2.1 says: "Don't use protected third-party material such as trademarks, copyrighted works, or patented ideas in your app without permission, and don't include misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata in your app bundle or developer name. Apps should be submitted by the person or legal entity that owns or has licensed the intellectual property and other relevant rights." — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Guideline 5.2 says: "Make sure your app only includes content that you created or that you have a license to use. Your app may be removed if you've stepped over the line..." Rights holders can file claims through Apple's dispute web form. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.2.2: if your app displays content from a third-party service, you must be permitted under that service's terms. 5.2.3: no downloading or saving media from YouTube or Vimeo without authorization. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.2.5: Activity rings must not visualize Move/Exercise/Stand data in a way that resembles the Activity control. This matters to DOGFOOD's ring UI. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Developers report 5.2.1 rejections over third-party branding, e.g. club logos and names. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 718427](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/718427); [thread 697240](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/697240)
- Lanham Act §43(a) (15 U.S.C. §1125(a)) creates a false-endorsement claim for using "any word, name, term, symbol..." in a way likely to confuse people about affiliation or sponsorship. Right of publicity is a separate state-law claim. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/protecting-image-and-likeness-through-trademark-law); [Trademark Engine](https://www.trademarkengine.com/blog/false-endorsement-unfair-competition/)
- Animal Flow's own rules for instructors: "mixed modality classes are allowed on your app, but you cannot label it as an Animal Flow class." The logo may not be altered. — [Animal Flow Online Content Guidelines](https://animalflow.com/af-online-content-guidelines/) (via search snippet)
- CrossFit says descriptive use of its term to describe workouts is not barred, which is the general nominative/descriptive line. — [CrossFit](https://games.crossfit.com/article/whats-name)
- Apple 1.4.1 (medical): apps must disclose methodology for health-measurement accuracy claims and should remind users to consult a doctor. This is relevant if mobility content is framed as rehab. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
Suggested naming rules for DOGFOOD:
1. **Use generic, descriptive names:** body position plus action plus animal analogy where it is already common usage (bear crawl, crab walk, lizard crawl, frog squat, duck walk, inchworm, gorilla hop, deep squat hang). Write your own names for transitions rather than "Underswitch" or "Scorpion Reach".
2. **Keep system names out of the app:** no "Animal Flow", "MovNat", "GMB", "Elements", "Original Strength", "Pressing Reset", "Ido Portal" or "Movement Culture" in the app name, subtitle, keywords, In-App Purchase names, session titles, or push copy. Note that App Store keyword stuffing with competitor marks is a classic 5.2.1 rejection pattern.
3. **No people's names as labels:** no "Ido Portal squat routine" or "Mike Fitch flow". Using a living coach's name as a product label invites both §43(a) false endorsement and state right-of-publicity claims. Factual historical attribution in an educational article ("ground-based training has been popularised by systems such as ...") is lower risk if it is plainly informational, has no logos, and includes a non-affiliation disclaimer. Even that is optional. The simplest choice is no mention at all.
4. **Avoid look-alike branding:** no crouching-monkey icon resembling the IDOPORTAL design mark, and no AF-style logo.
5. **Use your own media only:** no YouTube embeds of system creators unless their terms permit it (5.2.2/5.2.3).
6. **Frame mobility content as training, not treatment,** to stay clear of 1.4.1 scrutiny.

### Gaps
- No case law found on "inspired by [system]" labeling in fitness apps, or on nominative fair use for a trainer's name in an app. A *New Kids* (9th Cir.) analysis would apply but was not researched here.
- Apple's handling of competitor names in App Store *keywords* specifically was not verified this session.

## What existing apps do with animal and ground movement, and how they present it

### Takeaway
The dedicated systems (GMB Elements, Animal Flow OnDemand) win on teaching quality and clear named progressions. They lose on app stability, short or unstructured flows, and billing complaints. Calisthenics apps (Movement Athlete, Thenx, Caliverse) win on assessment-driven personalization and skill trees, but largely ignore locomotion. Mainstream apps (NTC, Fitness+, Fitbod) treat animal moves as occasional warm-ups or conditioning. DOGFOOD has an opening for structured, dosed locomotion that plugs into existing strength and running plans and adapts to readiness.

## Licensing options: certification and content partnerships

### Takeaway
All the major systems run instructor certifications. Only Animal Flow publishes rules for app and online use, and they allow certified instructors to feature AF in up to about 10% of app content without a general license. Anything more, including branded classes, a country account or merchandise, needs a signed license. Certification licenses an individual instructor. It is not a content license for a company's app.

### Cited Findings
- Animal Flow rules (via search snippets of the official guidelines page):
  - Up to 10% of app content can feature Animal Flow without a general licensing contract.
  - Mixed-modality classes are allowed in an app but cannot be labeled as Animal Flow classes.
  - The logo may not be altered.
  - Official country accounts need approval and a signed contract with trademark terms.
  - Branded merchandise needs a licensing contract; the listed fee is $4 per shirt.
  - Questions go to a listed contact address.
  — [Animal Flow Online Content Guidelines](https://animalflow.com/af-online-content-guidelines/)
- Animal Flow certification requires a video practical exam graded by a Master Instructor. Attending the workshop alone does not make you certified. Regional Leaders cannot run "workshops" or retreats under the AF name without HQ coordination. — [AF L1 Test Out Instructions](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Animal-Flow-L1-Test-Out-Instructions.pdf); [AF Regional Leader Application](https://animalflow.com/regional-leader-application/)
- Affiliate studios use "ANIMAL FLOW®... used under license". — [Primal MKE](https://www.primalmke.com/pages/animal-flow-at-primal)
- MovNat offers MCT Levels 1–3, with CEU approval from NCSF, NASM and AFAA, a fully private option, and an MCT renewal path. Level 2 is a "teacher level" credential with a competence test. — [MovNat Level 2](https://movnat.com/certification/level-two/)
- Original Strength runs an institute and education entity, suggesting a certification business (Original Strength Institute, Inc.; Class 41 education registrations). — [Justia: Original Strength Institute](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/original-strength-institute-inc-3306029); [Justia: ORIGINAL STRENGTH](https://trademarks.justia.com/858/53/original-85853559.html)
- GMB's trademark filings cover seminars, personal training and a fitness app, indicating it is its own app/content business rather than a licensor. — [Justia owner page, Super 301](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/super-301-inc-3324599)

### Inferences
- **Option A (recommended for a small app):** no license. Generic names, in-house video, original programming. Lowest cost. Legally sound under *Bikram* and the 2012 policy, provided the naming rules above are followed.
- **Option B:** hire or partner with a certified AF/MovNat/OS instructor to film original DOGFOOD content under generic names. Certification doesn't give DOGFOOD rights to the brand. Under AF's rules the content can't be labeled "Animal Flow" without a license. The 10% allowance appears aimed at instructors' own apps, not third-party companies, so confirm in writing.
- **Option C:** a formal co-branded license (e.g. "Animal Flow® in DOGFOOD"). This would need direct negotiation with GBT (Animal Flow) or MovNat. No public rate card for app content licensing was found.
