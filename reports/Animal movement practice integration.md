# Slot ground movement into training, not around it

The best way for DOGFOOD to add Ido Portal-style and animal/quadrupedal movement is as a **small, generically named library of crawls, holds, transitions and floor skills that plugs into the slots the app already has**. That means 3–6 minutes inside the warm-up Move/Prime stages, a few minutes in the cooldown Move stage, a 10–20 minute rest-day flow, and two daily "accumulation" skills: the resting deep squat and the hang. Each movement climbs a quality-gated ladder, and the readiness (Charge) and injury logic adjusts it. It should not become a separate programme bolted on beside lifting and running. The science supports a modest pitch, not a transformative one. About three small controlled trials, mostly from one research group with ties to the Animal Flow brand, show that quadrupedal training is **moderate-intensity exercise (about 5.4 METs)** and improved **movement-screen scores and 7 of 18 hip and shoulder range-of-motion measures** over 8 weeks. The same trials found **no gain in balance, grip or push-up endurance**. No trial supports the popular claims about squat-resting, hanging for shoulder "decompression", or mobility "snacks". The best-supported finding in the whole area is a harm signal: **two or more hours a day of habitual squatting is linked to roughly double the odds of knee osteoarthritis**. Every system studied progresses by movement quality and automaticity rather than fixed numbers. So the app's doses and pass criteria have to be coaching synthesis, which this report flags and which a coach or physiotherapist should review before launch. Legally, the movements themselves are free to teach, because exercises and sequences are not copyrightable in the US. The system names (ANIMAL FLOW, MOVNAT, ORIGINAL STRENGTH, GMB FITNESS, GINASTICA NATURAL) and living coaches' names are not free, and Apple's guideline 5.2.1 enforces that in store metadata. The market gap is real. Dedicated movement apps teach well but don't connect to a training plan, and mainstream strength apps treat crawling as an occasional warm-up novelty. No app found dosed locomotion inside an adaptive strength-and-running plan.

> **Research limitation.** Researchers could not open most primary pages in full. Network restrictions blocked full-text access to PubMed, PMC, ScienceDirect, the USPTO's trademark database (TSDR) and the official sites of Animal Flow, GMB, Ido Portal, Original Strength and MovNat. Findings therefore come from abstracts, search-result snippets, repository records and secondary summaries. Treat effect sizes, sample sizes, trademark statuses and dosing numbers as "as reported in an abstract or snippet". Before any number is quoted in app copy, check it against the original paper or the live trademark record. Throughout, **[E]** marks peer-reviewed evidence, **[P]** marks a documented system or practitioner convention, and **[S]** marks DOGFOOD-specific synthesis or coaching opinion that no source supplied.

## Three small trials carry the entire quadrupedal evidence base

The literature on adult quadrupedal movement training (QMT) is thin and concentrated. The anchor is a JSCR randomised trial by Buxton and colleagues. It randomised **42 college-age adults** to an Animal Flow-based programme (60-minute classes, twice a week for 8 weeks) or to control. The QMT group improved its Functional Movement Screen (FMS) composite by **1.62 ± 1.53 points versus 0.33 ± 1.15** for control (p=0.004). It also improved more than control on **7 of 18 active range-of-motion measures**, mostly at the hips and shoulders ([PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345976136_The_Effects_of_a_Novel_Quadrupedal_Movement_Training_Program_on_Functional_Movement_Range_of_Motion_Muscular_Strength_and_Endurance)). The same trial found **no between-group differences in Y-Balance, handgrip strength or push-up endurance** ([PubMed 33021583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/)). A companion energy-cost study of 30 adults measured a 60-minute beginner class at **5.4 ± 1.0 METs and HR 127 bpm**, which meets ACSM's moderate-intensity criteria. Walking matched to the same heart rate burned more energy (7.6 vs 6.7 kcal/min) ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.992687)).

The cognition claim rests on Matthews et al. (2016). In **22 sports-science students**, 4 weeks of progressive quadrupedal gait practice improved Wisconsin Card Sorting Task errors. Joint-position sense improved only at the 20° shoulder angle that was trained ([Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning)). The control group did no comparable novel motor task. The effect may therefore reflect learning any new complex skill rather than anything specific to moving on all fours. A December 2025 basketball RCT added Animal Flow twice a week after the warm-up for 12 weeks. Its abstract claims gains in agility, core endurance and repeated sprints, but another snippet says only functional movement differed between groups, and the sample size was not retrievable ([DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/psbd/article/1672553)). Researchers should also note a name collision: "QMT" also stands for *Quadrato* Motor Training, an unrelated standing step pattern with its own cognition literature ([PMC4511076](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4511076/)). The brand's own science page calls adult quadrupedal research "a newer field" and lists essentially these same papers ([Animal Flow science page](https://animalflow.com/science_of_animalflow/)). The overlap between researchers and brand, together with the small samples, leaves allegiance and publication bias open.

Acute EMG work supports using crawling for trunk training in the warm-up, without making it a superior shoulder exercise. Travelling bear crawls and bear limb-lifts activated every trunk muscle tested more than a static bear hold did ([ResearchGate, Pyka et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318471304_Effects_of_Static_Stationary_and_Traveling_Trunk_Exercises_on_Muscle_Activation)). A 2024 study found QMT exercises produced activation comparable to or higher than push-ups, squats and forearm planks ([OUCI record, Buxton et al. 2024](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40NvVvP9)). A shoulder EMG study reported **greater lower and middle trapezius activity in the push-up plus than in the bear crawl**, although only the abstract was visible ([APJHS](https://apjhs.com/index.php/apjhs/article/download/1541/1185)). For floor skills more generally, the Turkish get-up places peak shoulder demand at stage 2 (press to elbow) and stage 5 (leg sweep) ([PubMed 30691756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30691756/)). In a cohort of 2,002 adults aged 51–80, each extra point on the 0–10 sitting-rising test went with about **21% lower mortality** over 6.3 years ([SAGE, Brito/Araújo et al. 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/2047487312471759)). That association supports teaching floor transitions as a functional skill. It does not show that practising them extends life.

The popular "accumulation" practices have the weakest support. No randomised trial was found showing that deep-squat resting improves hip or ankle mobility. In the Beijing Osteoarthritis Study, people who squatted **120 minutes or more a day at age 25** had adjusted odds ratios of **2.0 (men) and 2.4 (women)** for tibiofemoral knee OA compared with those who squatted under 30 minutes ([BVS/Chinese J Epidemiol](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/gim/resource/ar/wpr-232325); [Scilit, Zhang 2004](https://scilit.com/publications/a91d0d4277e2257d710b2c43d9146f84)). A 16-study occupational meta-analysis put squatting's knee-OA odds ratio at **1.9** ([OARSI](https://www.oarsijournal.com/article/S1063-4584(11)00067-7/pdf)). The same Beijing population has very low hip-OA prevalence (about 1% versus about 4% in US whites), but squatting has never been shown to explain it ([GHDx](https://ghdx.healthdata.org/node/114971)). For hanging, Kirsch's claims that it remodels the acromion and cures impingement come from a self-published book and an unpublished case series ([APTEI](https://www.aptei.ca/library-article/single-exercise-to-cure-rotator-cuff-impingement-syndrome/)). Circulating figures such as "1–2 mm more subacromial space" could not be traced to any primary paper ([Gripp](https://www.gripp.fit/blog/dead-hangs-for-shoulder-pain-what-the-research-actually-says)). Grip strength is a strong *prognostic marker*: in PURE, every 5 kg lower grip went with an all-cause mortality hazard ratio of **1.16** ([PubMed 25982160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov:443/25982160)). The authors themselves say trials are needed to show that raising grip strength lowers risk. "Exercise snacks" (bouts of 5 minutes or less, at least twice a day) improved cardiorespiratory fitness in inactive adults with **g=1.37** (11 RCTs, n=414, I²=71%, moderate certainty). They had no effect on strength, body composition, blood pressure or lipids ([BMJ Group](https://bmjgroup.com/exercise-snacks-may-boost-cardiorespiratory-fitness-of-physically-inactive-adults/)). Later coverage reported the VO2max effect losing significance after adjustment for publication bias ([News-Medical](https://news-medical.net/news/20261006/Short-bursts-of-exercise-may-improve-fitness-and-blood-sugar-in-sedentary-adults.aspx)). Those trials tested vigorous snacks such as stair climbing, not mobility snacks.

### Evidence grades for the claims DOGFOOD might make

Grades: **Strong** means several consistent RCTs or meta-analyses, or very large cohorts for associations. **Moderate** means one or two decent RCTs, or a heterogeneous meta-analysis. **Weak** means small single trials, acute EMG or cross-sectional data. **Expert opinion** means practitioner, book or anecdote only.

| Claim | Best evidence | Grade | App may say? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground/animal movement is moderate-intensity exercise | n=30 acute study, 5.4 METs ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.992687)) | Moderate (acute) | **Yes**: "moderate-intensity full-body movement" |
| Improves movement-screen scores and some hip/shoulder mobility | One RCT, n=42, 8 wk ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33021583/)) | Weak–Moderate | **Yes, qualified**: "small trials found improved movement quality and some hip and shoulder mobility" |

| Improves cognition / "rewires the brain" | n=22, no active control ([Salford](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1404114/quadrupedal-movement-training-improves-markers-of-cognition-and-joint-repositioning)) | Weak | **No** neuroplasticity language. At most: "learning new skills is mentally engaging" |

| Mobility "movement snacks" improve health | None | Expert opinion | **No** health claim. "Breaks up sitting; practises skills" is fine |
| Crawling is safe for wrists and shoulders | No injury-surveillance data anywhere | None | Neither claim nor deny. Design conservatively |

The copy-safe vocabulary is therefore narrow: moderate-intensity movement, skill practice, coordination challenge, trunk work, floor-transition practice, grip training, and "small studies suggest better movement quality and some mobility." The app should also follow Apple guideline 1.4.1 by presenting all of this as training rather than treatment, so mobility content does not read as rehabilitation or medical advice ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

## Every established system progresses by quality, not by stopwatch

Across six systems, the curricula converge on one skeleton: **joint prep (especially wrists), static positions, travelling patterns, transitions, then freeform combinations**. They also converge on a progression rule: advance when a pattern is clean and automatic, and step back when it breaks down. None of the reachable official material published hold-time or distance gates such as "hold 30 seconds before travelling". The most concrete numbers are Ido Portal's daily accumulation challenges and Original Strength's per-reset minutes.

Two dosing models fall out of this table, and DOGFOOD needs both. **Bounded blocks** (GMB, MovNat, Animal Flow) run 10–45 minutes and fit the app's session structure. **Accumulation targets** (Portal) are daily minute totals spread across the day, which fit a "movement snack" progress ring. The warm-up literature supplies the placement logic that the systems lack. DOGFOOD's Raise/Move/Prime stages mirror Jeffreys' RAMP model (Raise → Activate & Mobilise → Potentiate), which the UKSCA recommends and which matches warm-up content to the demands of the session ([Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/strength-conditioning-fitness/the-ramp-system)). Warm-ups improved performance on **79% of criteria** in a 32-study meta-analysis ([PubMed 19996770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19996770/)), and brief warm-ups do best ([Sports Med 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs40279-015-0376-x)). Fatigue is the counterweight. Core pre-fatigue reduced knee flexion and increased knee valgus in squats among 16 female bodybuilders ([DOAJ 2022](https://doaj.org/article/58d670ffd44048ab8b074bd06b54415d)), and whole-body pre-fatigue altered squat kinematics in 21 athletes ([PLOS ONE 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10681224/)). Static stretching costs a little maximal strength, least at 45 seconds or less per muscle ([Simic 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22316148/)). Pre-lift crawling should therefore be positional, skill-level exposure. Crawl conditioning belongs after lifting or on separate days. For running, a 2025 systematic review (15 studies, low certainty) found stretching type did not acutely change running economy ([Sports Medicine – Open](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-025-00859-0)). No source addressed movement practice before runs, so the run-day placements below are synthesis.

## A 20-movement library mapped onto DOGFOOD's existing slots

The library below uses **DOGFOOD-safe generic names** (the commonly known equivalent is in brackets for internal reference only; see the IP section). It is ordered along the cross-system progression that the EMG data also support: static → stationary limb-lift → travelling → linked/loaded. **Every dose, level and pass criterion in these tables is [S] coaching synthesis unless a citation is attached.** These values follow coaching convention and the documented principles above, but no source validated them. A qualified coach or physiotherapist should review them before shipping. Slot codes: **W-Move** = warm-up Move, **W-Prime** = warm-up Prime, **Pre-run**, **CD** = cooldown Move, **Hold** = cooldown Hold/PNF, **Rest** = rest-day flow, **Snack** = daily 2–5 min movement snack, **Acc** = daily accumulation skill.

The value-to-risk ratio is poor |

### Accumulation skills and the overhead line

The two Portal-style accumulators need different defaults from the original challenges. The 30-minute squat target is a quarter of the ≥2 h/day exposure linked to knee OA. That exposure, though, was lifetime habitual squatting, not short training holds. For a running population with a higher rate of knee complaints, DOGFOOD should **default to a modest daily target and offer the full challenge as an opt-in**.

| Skill | Purpose · slots | Default daily target [S] | Per-bout quality levels and pass criterion [S] | Contraindications / modifications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Resting deep squat** (squat rest, prying) | Squat comfort and a place to rest; breaks up sitting. Acc, Snack, W-Move on squat days, CD/Hold | Default ramp: **2 → 5 → 10 min/day accumulated**. Opt-in "30-day, 30-minute" challenge after 2 weeks at 10 min. ### Session-type menus and time budgets

| Session | Raise | Move (ground drills) | Prime | Ground-work budget [S] | Avoid beforehand [S] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Squat day** | 2–4 min bike/row | Rock-back, deep-squat pry, cossack, spiderman | Hover 2 × 15 s (bracing rehearsal), then empty-bar ramp sets | 3–6 min | Long squat-rest holds, frog-hop volume |
| **Deadlift day** | 2–4 min | Walk-out, rock-back | Bear crawl 1 × 5 m, hollow 1 × 15 s | 3–5 min | Long hamstring static holds; crawl conditioning |
| **Bench / overhead press** | 2–4 min | Wrist prep, crab bridge reach | Side bear crawl or hover shoulder taps; half get-up. Wall line on overhead days for eligible users | 3–6 min | Lizard crawl; long hollow holds |
| **Pull-up day** | 2–4 min | Crab reach, hollow 2 × 15 s | Scap pulls, active hang 2 × 10 s | 3–5 min | Passive hangs >30 s (grip fatigue) |
| **Easy run** | 3–5 min easy jog | Spiderman, cossack, walk-out | Short bear crawl forward/back; 2–3 frog hops or traverses; then strides | 2–4 min | Nothing heavy; dynamic work is neutral to positive ([PubMed 25932984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25932984/)) |
| **Intervals / race** | Standard jog | Spiderman, cossack only | Strides and drills; no crawling | ≤2 min | Any crawl or hop volume |
| **Cooldown** | Roll 1–3 min | Crab reach, rock-back, rolls (2–3 min) | Hold/PNF 2–4 min: squat rest, passive hang 30–60 s if eligible | 4–7 min | — (static holds of 30–60 s are fine here) |
| **Rest day** | — | 10–20 min ground flow | — | 10–20 min | Conditioning intensity |
| **Snack** | — | 1–3 drills (wrist prep, squat rest, hang, 90/90, rolls) | — | 2–5 min | — |

The rule behind these budgets [S]: within a typical 8–12 minute lifting warm-up, about 3–6 minutes of ground work fits without fatiguing the lifter. Anything that leaves the trunk or grip tired, such as timed crawl ladders, high-rep hops or holds over 30 seconds, moves after the main work. Running tolerates more, except before quality sessions. The app should also keep accumulated squat-rest minutes away from the 30 minutes before a heavy squat session. This is a precaution, because no source tested interference.

## Charge should shrink ground work to positions, not delete it

No study has tested readiness-adjusted movement dosing. The warm-up literature does show that excess intensity, excess duration and too little recovery impair short-term performance ([Bishop 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12762825/)), and that pre-fatigue alters squat mechanics ([DOAJ 2022](https://doaj.org/article/58d670ffd44048ab8b074bd06b54415d)). Every system's own safety rule points the same way: Animal Flow says stop when form breaks down ([Animal Flow](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/understanding-the-6-components-of-animal-flow/)), and GMB drops a level when a skill breaks ([GMB](https://gmb.io/feed/)). That supports one principle for DOGFOOD [S]. **Low Charge converts ground work from conditioning and potentiation into low-intensity positional practice. It is the last part of a session to cut, because it is the cheapest to recover from.** When Charge trims or swaps a lift session, the recovery flow is the natural replacement. When Charge is low, the app should never add crawl conditioning to "make up" lost volume.

| Charge band [S] | Warm-up ground work | Finishers | Rest-day / swap option | Accumulators | Progression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | Full Move + Prime, including potentiation (frog hops, short bear crawls, hover taps) | Crawl conditioning allowed after lifting (3–5 × 20 m bear crawl, or a combo of 3–5 rounds in under 20 min, after MovNat's format ([MovNat](https://movnat.com/beginner-movnat-combo-workout-vol-1/))) | Full 20-min flow | Full target | Level tests allowed |
| **Moderate** | Standard menu | Halve or drop | 15-min flow | Full target | Level tests allowed |
| **Low** | Swap travelling and plyometric moves for positional versions (hover instead of bear crawl; squat pry instead of frog hop) | None | **Swap target:** 10–15 min recovery flow (rock-back, rolls, crab reach, spiderman, slow switches) at RPE ≤3–4 with nasal breathing | Target kept at current level; no new levels | **No level tests** |
| **Very low / unwell** | Snacks only | None | Wrist prep, squat rest, active hang, 90/90 | Target reduced (e.g. 50%), with streak protected | Frozen |

Two design rules follow [S]. First, **progression gating**: unlock the next level only when the pass criterion is met in two separate sessions at Moderate-or-better Charge. Drop back one level automatically when a logged attempt fails the quality check. This is GMB's rule made explicit, and it also matches the app's existing mobility and strength skill levels. Second, **streak design must not fight Charge**. Portal's 30/30 has no rest days ([Ignore Limits](https://ignorelimits.com/ido-portal-3030-squat-challenge/)). Within DOGFOOD, a low-Charge day should count a reduced "touch" toward the streak, so a user isn't pushed to grind out a full target while depleted. If the accumulator shows a progress ring, Apple guideline 5.2.5 bars visuals that resemble the Activity rings ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

### Injury flags propagate through movement tags

The injury-aware swap system works best if every movement carries load tags [S]: **hand-loaded (wrist extension), hanging (passive/active), deep knee flexion, impact/jump, inverted/head-down, supine, overhead loaded, rotational speed**. A logged flag then removes or substitutes every tagged item at once. The rules below are mostly clinical reasoning and coaching consensus. The firmer anchors are the intraocular-pressure data, the meniscus-repair protocols and pregnancy guidance.

| Flag | Remove | Substitute / modify | Clearance rule [S] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wrist** | Wall handstand, lizard crawl, switches/transitions | Wrist prep is mandatory. Fists, parallettes or wedge for all hand-loaded moves; forearm hover/plank ([E3 Rehab](https://e3rehab.com/wrist-pain-rehab/)) | Restore after 2 pain-free sessions at the current level |
| **Shoulder: impingement / pain** | Wall handstand and deep crab reach if they reproduce pain | Keep scap pulls and active hang if pain-free; passive hang feet-supported, 10–20 s cap; prefer bear and hover positions below 90° flexion | Pain-free over 2 sessions |
| **Shoulder: instability / hypermobility** | Passive hangs, crab-reach end range | Active hangs only; slow, light get-ups ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.edu.au/what-are-dead-hangs-what-are-the-shoulder-pain-risks-and-how-do-i-do-them-safely-202346)) | Clinician-led |
| **Frozen shoulder (painful phase), recent dislocation, post-op shoulder** | All hanging and inverted work | Hover holds only if pain-free | Clinician-led ([Acibadem](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/dead-hangs-what-hanging-from-a-bar-does-for-shoulders-grip-and-spine-and-who-should-avoid-it/)) |
| **Knee: pain, OA, meniscus** | Duck walk, frog hop, side squat traverse, squat-rest below pain-free depth | Box-height or supported squat rest (≤5 min/day); half-range cossack; limited-range rock-back. High flexion loads the posterior meniscus ([PMC8689273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689273)) | Depth restored in steps of one level |
| **Knee: post-op** | All deep loaded flexion | Follow the surgeon's protocol. HSS restricts end-range closed-chain flexion until about week 13 ([HSS](https://www.hss.edu/files/HSSRehabilitationClinicalGuidelines-Knee-MeniscusRepair-Post-operative.pdf)) | Clinician clearance |
| **Low back** | Loaded crawls, long arch holds, fast switches | Bird dog, hover, hollow regressions, rock-back within pain-free range (no sources; clinical reasoning) | Pain-free over 2 sessions |
| **Glaucoma / ocular hypertension / uncontrolled hypertension** | Wall handstand, prolonged head-down positions | Shortened walk-out; avoid long breath-hold bracing. IOP rises within a minute of head-down yoga poses ([PLOS ONE 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4689525)) | Permanent unless a clinician clears it |
| **Pregnancy** | Inversions, jumps (frog hop), switches, L5 rolls, long supine holds after trimester 1 | Rock-back, hover if comfortable, side-lying work, supported squat rest, supported cossack. ACOG flags fall risk and the supine position ([MotherToBaby](https://mothertobaby.org/fact-sheets/exercise-pregnancy/); [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430821/)) | Postpartum: restart at L1 with clinician guidance |
| **Older adult (opt-in profile)** | Jumps until balance criteria are met; duck walk | Start with rolls, rock-back, bird dog, supported squat rest, half get-up, floor sit-and-rise practice. Screen for osteoporosis (fall risk getting to the floor) | Progress by the same quality gates. No QMT trial exists in older adults ([Animal Flow science](https://animalflow.com/flowist_post/science-behind-animal-flow/)) |

Universal pain rules should sit above the flags [S]. Stand up and move if the knees or hips hurt during squat rest, as Portal-style guidance already says ([Ignore Limits](https://ignorelimits.com/ido-portal-3030-squat-challenge/)). Stop any drill when form breaks. Treat sharp pain as a stop signal, never a cue to push through ([GMB wrists](https://gmb.io/wrists/)). Ask about next-day ache, which also serves as the wrist and knee pass criterion. Whether to train into pain is still unsettled even in rotator-cuff rehabilitation trials ([PMC10880378](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10880378/)). A consumer app should therefore default to pain-avoidance.

## Generic movement names are free; system names and coaches' names are not

US copyright law does not protect exercises or their sequences. In *Bikram's Yoga College v. Evolation Yoga* (9th Cir. 2015), the Ninth Circuit held that a 26-pose sequence is an unprotectable "idea, process or system" and not a choreographic work ([FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-9th-circuit/1715117.html)). The Copyright Office's 2012 policy statement says "a compilation of exercises would not be copyrightable subject matter" ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2012-06-22/html/2012-15235.htm)). DOGFOOD can therefore teach bear crawls, crab reaches and lateral squat traverses, and can even build progressions that resemble another system's. What remains protected is **expression**: videos, photos, illustrations, manual text and cue scripts, logos and music. Animal Flow's Level 1 and Level 2 manuals are copyrighted documents ([AF L1 Manual](https://animalflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Animal-Flow-Level-1-Manual-English-2023.pdf)). Contract can also restrict what copyright doesn't. Any team member who took a certification or bought a programme may be bound by its terms, so they should not paraphrase course material into the app.

Trademark is where the real constraints sit. Statuses below come from aggregator snippets and need TSDR confirmation.

Individual animal words (beast, ape, crab) are not registered as movement names. As a set, though, "the ABCs", "Loaded Beast", "Underswitch", "Scorpion Reach" and "Side Kickthrough" signal Animal Flow, and common-law rights are plausible. The safe practice is to **use body-position-plus-action names in common usage (bear crawl, crab walk, lizard crawl, frog hop, walk-out, deep squat) and coin DOGFOOD's own names for transitions** ("quadruped hover", "bear-to-crab switch", "hip-over reach"), as the library tables already do. Keep system names and people's names out of the app name, subtitle, keywords, in-app purchase names, session titles and push copy. Guideline 5.2.1 bars third-party trademarks and "copycat representations, names, or metadata" without permission ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and developers report rejections over third-party branding ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/718427)). CrossFit's own position marks the line: describing a workout is acceptable, but branding a product with someone else's mark is not ([CrossFit](https://games.crossfit.com/article/whats-name)). Guideline 5.2.3 also rules out re-hosting creators' YouTube or Vimeo videos. Licensing is optional. Animal Flow's guidelines let certified instructors feature its material in up to about 10% of app content without a general licence. That allowance appears aimed at instructors' own apps, and a branded partnership would need a signed contract ([AF Online Content Guidelines](https://animalflow.com/af-online-content-guidelines/)). For a small app, the sound default is no licence, generic names and in-house video.

## Ship the warm-up slots and accumulators first, flows last

Implementation should follow value-to-risk. The highest-value features reuse infrastructure DOGFOOD already has (warm-up stages, skill levels, Charge, injury swaps). Higher-skill flows, which carry the most authoring cost and the most wrist and shoulder exposure, come last.

## Conclusion

The research changes what this feature *is*. Ground movement is not a physiological shortcut DOGFOOD can sell on outcomes. Its proven effects are modest, and its most-cited benefits (brain rewiring, shoulder decompression, arthritis-proof hips) are unsupported. Its defensible value is as **coordination and positional skill practice that costs almost nothing to recover from**. That is exactly what an adaptive planner needs to fill warm-ups, low-Charge days and rest days without adding fatigue. The competitive advantage therefore lies in the integration logic (slots, load tags, Charge bands, quality gates) rather than in movement content, which anyone can legally teach and which the dedicated apps already film well.

The counterintuitive risk is not intellectual property, which generic naming and in-house video largely neutralise. It is the accumulation challenges imported uncritically. A "30 minutes of squatting every day, no rest days" target sits in the direction of the one robust harm signal in this literature, and it ignores the readiness logic that defines DOGFOOD. Conservative default targets, knee-aware caps, Charge-protected streaks and honest copy turn a viral challenge into a durable habit. The open questions are good candidates for DOGFOOD's own data, given user consent: whether low-Charge recovery flows change next-day readiness, and whether the warm-up crawl doses affect lift performance. No published study has measured either.
